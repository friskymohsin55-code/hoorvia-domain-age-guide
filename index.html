How Old Is That Domain? Reading a Domain Age Report Before You Trust a Website

Before you buy from a new website, send money to an unfamiliar seller, or trust a business you found online, check how long its domain has existed. A domain's age will not prove that a site is honest, but it is one of the hardest facts to fake — and one of the easiest to check.

A worked example: example.com

Take example.com as a worked example. A report for it shows four lines that are worth reading together:

  • Domain Age: 31 Years, 87 Days
  • Created: 1995-08-14
  • Updated: 2026-08-14
  • Expiry: 2027-08-13

Those four lines, read together, tell a much clearer story than the age alone. To get them, run the domain through a Domain Age Checker.

Where the age comes from

Domain age comes from the registry record, retrieved through RDAP, the modern replacement for older WHOIS lookups. The creation date is set when the domain is first registered and cannot be edited afterwards to make a site look older. A seller can redesign a website, rewrite its history page, and invent customer reviews, but it cannot backdate that first registration.

Read all four lines together

The creation date gives the starting point, the age turns it into a simple number, the updated date shows when the record was last changed — in this case on 2026-08-14 — and the expiry date shows how far ahead the owner has paid. A domain renewed until 2027-08-13 suggests the owner expects the site to still be there next year. A domain expiring in a few weeks, by contrast, may be a short-term project.

When a claim does not match the dates

Use the same test on any business. If a company claims a decade of trading, a long history, and years of experience, but its domain is only six months old, that mismatch deserves a second look. It does not prove fraud — a business can move to a new domain — but it does mean you should ask for more evidence: check its address, read independent reviews, and confirm its details with a WHOIS Checker before you pay.

A quick three-step check

Start with the Domain Age Checker, then use the WHOIS Checker to see the registration details behind the dates, and the Backlink Checker to see which other sites link to it. Together, those checks take a few minutes and give you a far better sense of whether a website has the history it claims.
